# The ledger

Accounts, transactions, postings and the rules around them: integrity, dates, currency, depth, built-in groups, cards, recurring rules, related entries and tags. Dates in headings are when a decision was made. Moved out of CLAUDE.md on 2026-11-07; the rules summary is in `AGENTS.md`.

## Double-entry ledger

`app/models/account.py` defines accounts as a tree. Each user has five system
root accounts (`Assets`, `Liabilities`, `Equity`, `Income`, `Expenses`),
created lazily on first access by `AccountService._ensure_roots` (a partial
unique index on `(user, name)` where `parent_id IS NULL` keeps this race-safe).
There is no stored type: an account's type is its root ancestor, and all
non-root accounts require a `parent_id`. Roots cannot be renamed, moved, or
deleted. A budget **category** is its own table (`budget_categories`, see "Budget categories" in [budget.md](budget.md)); an Income or Expenses account is the books' side of one.

`app/models/transaction.py` defines `Transaction` and `Posting`. A transaction
contains at least two postings, every posting has a positive amount, and total
debits must equal total credits. The service layer validates those invariants
and verifies that all referenced accounts belong to the authenticated user.
Postings may not target root accounts. Asset and liability accounts that have
sub-accounts cannot be posted to either (and cannot gain sub-accounts once they
have postings), but Income and Expenses groups can (`GROUP_POSTING_ROOT_NAMES`
in `app/services/account.py`). An account cannot be moved under a different
root (that would silently reclassify its history).

Account balances are derived from postings. Assets and Expenses increase
with debits; Liabilities, Equity, and Income increase with credits. Asset and
liability accounts that already have postings cannot gain sub-accounts. Opening balances and
direct balance edits are posted against the system equity account named
`Opening Balances`, which cannot be renamed, moved, deleted, given
sub-accounts or have its balance set directly.

## Edges of what is accepted

Checked by `tests/test_ledger_edges.py`, and by `tests/test_ledger_fuzz.py` (random sequences of operations, then every balance recounted from the stored postings, the trial balance, a backup and restore, and the budget's identity `ready_to_assign = money_in_accounts - reserved_for_cards - available - assigned_to_later_months`). **Dates**: every day a person can enter (a transaction, a recurring rule's start and end, a budget month, the same fields in a backup) must lie between `clock.FIRST_DAY` (1900-01-01) and `LAST_DAY` (2999-12-31): use the `Day` type in `schemas/day.py` for a new date field. Past either end the calendar arithmetic ("the month before") could fail at year 1 or 9999, and one entry dated 9999-12-31 used to turn every page into an error. `BudgetService.month_start` refuses a month outside the span (`BudgetError`, 400 in the API); `first_of_month` is the unchecked version for dates already stored. **Computed figures** (balances, totals, the budget's figures: the `Read` models) have no digit limits, because a sum of valid amounts may be longer than one amount can be (a limit there made every page an error); only an input amount is limited (16 digits before the point, 8 after, then the currency's decimals). **Text**: a NUL character cannot go into Postgres, so `models/utils` drops it from every string on every write (a `before_flush` hook) and `TransactionRepository.list` from the search text. An account's name is trimmed, visible, on one line and without `:` (`schemas/account.clean_name`), and **unique among its siblings, capitals aside** (`AccountService._check_name_free`; a journal import is case-sensitive like the format and may still create `Food` and `food`); a payee is one tidy line and a blank payee or note is none. **One entry, one direction**: an account may not be on both the debit and the credit side of one transaction (`TransactionService._resolve`; several rows of the same side are fine). **Budget**: `assign` may be negative to take back what an earlier month left in a category (that is what Move money writes too) but never more than the category has left (`BudgetService.assign`), otherwise Ready to assign would exceed the money there is.

## Ledger integrity

- **Database checks:** `postings` has `CHECK` constraints (`amount > 0`, `value > 0`, `side IN ('DEBIT','CREDIT')`), added by migration `a3c5e7f9b124`, which refuses to run if existing rows break them. Balance and "at least two postings" cannot be a plain check, so they live in `TransactionService._resolve`; `ReportService.trial_balance` (`/api/reports/trial-balance`, MCP `check_books`) re-checks every transaction from the stored rows and names the ones that fail. Any new code path that writes transactions must keep a test asserting `trial_balance().balanced`.
- **Posting ids are stable:** `TransactionRepository._replace_postings` updates postings in place on edit (matching first on account and side, then on account), adding or removing only what is left, so anything that refers to a posting keeps working.
- **No history (2026-11-05):** nothing records what happened to a transaction or account; see [removed.md](removed.md). Only `created_via` / `updated_via` say who made or last changed an entry.
- **Balances by date:** account balances count postings dated up to `as_of` (today by default, so future-dated entries do not count yet; `?as_of=` on `/api/accounts`). The register still lists every entry.

## One currency

Everyone keeps their whole ledger in **one currency**: `profiles.currency` (an ISO 4217 code, INR for new people; migration `f4b6d8a0c2e5`), chosen in Settings › Currency (`POST /settings/currency`, `PATCH /api/profile/` with `default_currency`, the field name kept for API clients). Changing it **relabels, never converts**: every stored amount stays as it is (the page says so). There are no other currencies, no assets of one's own (shares, funds, coins), no prices and no conversions; the `commodities` and `prices` tables, `accounts.commodity_id`, `transactions.currency_id` and `postings.value` were dropped. The currency list is `app/currencies.py` (`CURRENCIES`, `find`, each with its decimals and sign); `AccountService.currency()` and `TransactionService.currency()` read it for the person, and `check_places` / `TransactionService._resolve` refuse amounts with more decimals than it allows (rupees 2, yen 0, dinars 3). `Amount` columns stay `Numeric(24,8)` (read back without padding zeros: do not format money with `:.2f`, use the `money` filter or `normalize_amount`). A posting is just an `amount`; a transaction balances when debits equal credits. `migrations/commodity_seed.py` is a frozen copy of the ISO list that only the early migrations use; the single-currency migration refuses (changing nothing) when somebody recorded assets of their own, prices, more than one currency or a conversion, and **cannot be undone** (restore a backup instead).

## Two levels

Accounts go **two levels below a top-level account, for everybody and every type**: `Expenses › Food › Groceries` is a category and a subcategory and the deepest there is (`MAX_ACCOUNT_DEPTH = 2` in `services/account.py`; it is not a setting and there is no per-person limit). `AccountService._check_depth` enforces it when creating or moving accounts (a moved subtree counts its own height, and a cycle is reported before depth), the account forms and the quick-account sheet hide parents with no room left (`_parent_options`), an account page offers "Add sub-account" only where there is room, `DataService._plan` refuses deeper imports with their line number and a restore refuses a deeper backup. Migration `a5c7e9b1d3f6` dropped `profiles.max_depth_*` and refuses to run (changing nothing) while an account is deeper; older backups' depth limits are read and ignored. Templates and the budget/categories trees therefore only ever nest a group once (a group's rows are all leaves). Any new place that creates accounts outside `AccountService` must check the depth itself.

## Naming (2026-10-31)

One convention everywhere (database, code, JSON API, URLs, assistants' output): **a table's primary key is `id`; a column or field that points at another table is `<entity>_id`** (`account_id`, `transaction_id`, `category_id`, `recurring_id`, `from_account_id`, `parent_id`, `related_id`); **the owner is `user_id`**. Path parameters and Python variables are named for what they are (`{account_id}`, `{transaction_id}`, `{token_id}`, `{grant_id}`), never a short code. Migration `b4d6f8a0c2e5` renamed the columns (only names changed; it can be undone); the plan and the table-by-table list are in `docs/developer/naming-plan.md`. The backup file (version 5) keeps its own readable keys (`account`, `category`) on purpose; a new table or field follows the convention above.

## Queries per page

The database is hosted (about 16 ms a round trip), so **a page must not query once per account or once per row**: that made the new-transaction page take 3.8 s (218 queries) until balances were computed from one query. Rules: list balances from `AccountService._postings_by_account` (one query for every posting, `list()` passes it to `_to_read`), lists of transactions load postings with `TransactionRepository.postings_for_transactions` and transactions with `read_many`, never `read(transaction_id)` or `postings(transaction_id)` in a loop. **The session remembers repeated reads** (`repositories/cache.py`, `remembered`): the account list, the profile, the budget plan, and postings are loaded once per request however many services ask, and forgotten on any write (flush, commit, rollback, a non-SELECT statement) and while the session holds unwritten changes, so a read never shows what was true before a write in the same session. Its results are shared: copy a list before sorting it in place. New repository reads that several services repeat belong there; a new write path needs nothing (flush and commit are hooked). In the test suite the shared session forgets at the start of each request (`conftest.client`, `tests/browser/conftest.py`), as each production request has its own. A transaction's postings are checked against one account list, never one query per posting. Responses are gzip-compressed (`caching.Compression`; fonts and images pass through). The budget reads the ledger through `TransactionRepository.posting_rows(date_to)` (postings joined to their date, no payees or notes, one query) rather than every transaction and every posting, and `ReportService.summary` reads income and expense postings in one query (2026-10-25). `tests/test_query_counts.py` counts the statements of the main pages with small and larger books, fails when the count grows with the data and holds each page under a ceiling (`CEILING`: lower it when a page gets cheaper, raise it only with a reason); `tests/test_read_memory.py` guards the memory. To measure on real data without touching production: restore the latest dump into a scratch Postgres, point `FINODE_DATABASE_URL` at it and count statements with an SQLAlchemy `before_cursor_execute` listener.

## What day it is

Never call `date.today()`: use `clock.local_today()` (and `local_now()`, `local_midnight_utc()` for the per-day limit). `FINODE_TIMEZONE` (an IANA name such as `Asia/Kolkata`, validated at start-up) is the zone "today" is read in for the whole installation, so entries, the recurring runner, the budget's current month and "Today" in lists follow the people's clock and not the server's; unset, the server's own clock is used. Timestamps stay in UTC. It is per installation, not per person: a person in another zone would need a profile column and the recurring runner would need each rule's owner's zone. Dependency `tzdata` is there so the slim Docker image has zone data.

## Recent and ordered accounts

**Order (2026-10-25):** accounts are listed alphabetically, the five built-in groups first in their fixed order (`AccountService.order_key`, `FIXED_ORDER`), by the accounts page, the pickers (`build_tree`) and the budget's category lines. There is no Move up/down and no stored order: `accounts.position` was dropped (migration `b2d4f6a8c0e3`; older backups' `position` is read and ignored). `accounts.pinned` was dropped earlier (`b6d8f0a2c4e7`) and came back as a different feature on 2026-11-10 (below).

## Built-in groups under Assets and Liabilities (2026-10-21)

Every **new** person starts with five groups that cannot be renamed, moved, closed or deleted (`accounts.system_key`, migration `b9d1f3a5c7e2`; `services/account.py` `FIXED_PARENTS`): **Bank Accounts** (`assets.bank`), **Cash** (`assets.cash`), **Investments** (`assets.investments`), **Credit Cards** (`liabilities.cards`), **Loans** (`liabilities.loans`), in that order (their `position`). They are made in the same transaction as the five roots (`_ensure_roots`, only when all five roots are missing, so **nobody who already has accounts gets them**; `NEW_PROFILE_FIXED_PARENTS`, which `tests/conftest.py` switches off and `tests/test_fixed_parents.py` switches on). Rules read the key, never the name. `AccountService._refuse_fixed` guards update (any field), close, delete and the placing of siblings (`FixedParentError`, a `SystemAccountError`, so the API answers 400); adding accounts under them, and moving an account into one, is allowed. **What is made under one starts as the group says** (`accounts.new_accounts_in_budget`, see "Groups the person makes"; `create`: only when the request did not give `on_budget`): Bank Accounts, Cash and Credit Cards part of the budget, Investments and Loans outside it; the person can still choose otherwise. Web: `build_tree` gives them `kind="fixed"` (not postable, no edit page, no menu but "Add sub-account"; no lock icon); **the Accounts page always shows the five built-in groups, empty ones too** (2026-11-10; before, a group appeared once it held an account; each shelf has its own dashed add card, so nothing else is needed to start); a group's own page is described in `ui-pages.md` (its accounts as cards, then their transactions; no menu for a built-in one); the form's Under options carry `data-start` and `budgetChoice` follows the group chosen; the "+" on a type starts on its first group. Backup carries `system_key`; **a restore maps the file's keyed groups onto the person's own, and an empty built-in group whose name the file uses for an account of its own (a plain Assets › Cash) steps aside**; a journal import does the same for a posting to such an account (`empty_fixed_parents`, `release_fixed_parents`; `can_restore` ignores empty keyed groups). Erasing the books gives the groups back (the roots are made again). The assistants' instructions mention them. The opt-in sheet for existing people (Settings › Standard groups) was removed 2026-10-25: the owner has adopted the groups already; nobody else has accounts from before them.

### Groups the person makes (2026-11-10)

A group is an account that holds accounts instead of entries, and it can stand empty. `accounts.new_accounts_in_budget` (migration `c6d8f0a2b4e7`) marks one: **set on a group, empty on every other account**. The value is what an account made in the group starts as (`create`, when the request gave no `on_budget`); the built-in groups carry what `FIXED_PARENTS` says. It is a column of its own, not the group's `on_budget`, because the budget code reads `on_budget` of every account and would count a group. A plain account that gained accounts of its own (made with "Add sub-account", the API or a restore) is still a group while it has them, but has no mark and keeps the type's default; `Node.is_group` (web) and `AccountService` treat both as groups.

`AccountService.create_group(GroupCreate)` / `POST /api/accounts/groups` / `GET|POST /accounts/new-group`: a name, the type it sits under (`Assets` for money the person owns, `Liabilities` for money they owe; fixed for life, so the form words it "Money I own / Money I owe"), and the choice. No balance, no note. Names are unique among siblings (so a group cannot be called Cash). `PATCH` changes the name and `new_accounts_in_budget` (the choice of an existing account is not touched; only a group takes it, and it cannot be cleared; built-in groups stay locked). A group takes no entries (`transaction.py` refuses a posting to anything marked or with accounts in it), is not offered in the posting pickers (`Node.postable`), and is not a budget candidate (`budget.py`). It is deleted like any account: while empty. Backup carries the field (files from before have none); a restore recreates empty groups.

### Pinned accounts (2026-11-10)

`accounts.pinned` (migration `d7e9a1c3b5f8`, false by default) lists an account again in a **Pinned** row at the top of the Accounts page; it changes no balance, budget or entry. Only an open asset or liability account that is not a group, a root, a contact's account or Opening Balances can be pinned (`AccountService._check_pin`, a 400 otherwise); taking a pin off is always allowed, and closing an account keeps its flag (the Pinned row hides closed accounts, so reopening brings it back). There is no order of its own: the row follows the order of the rows below it (no Move up/down). It is set by `PATCH /api/accounts/{account_id}` (`pinned`), the assistants' `update_account` (`pinned`; `list_accounts` shows `"pinned": true`) and the account page's menu (`POST /accounts/{account_id}/pin` and `/unpin`). The backup carries it (files from before may leave it out). The first version of this column (dropped by `b6d8f0a2c4e7`) kept no data; the old pins are gone.

## A card's bill (2026-11-01, `feature/card-bills`)

A liability account with no sub-accounts (a card, a loan) may carry a **statement day** and a **due day** (`accounts.statement_day`, `due_day`, 1 to 31, both or neither; migration `d8f0b2c4e6a9` with CHECKs; `AccountBase`/`AccountUpdate`, the account form's `data-bill-fields` (shown for a Liabilities parent, `bill_choice` marks the form so clearing works), backup (`BackupAccount`, still version 5), `update_account`/`create_account` tools). `services/cards.py` is the pure calendar (`last_statement`, `next_statement`, `due_date`: day 31 is the last day of a short month; a due day not later than the statement day falls next month). `AccountService.bill(account_id, today)` (`CardBill` in `schemas/account.py`; `GET /api/accounts/{account_id}/bill`, the tool `get_card_bill`) works the bill out from the account's postings (`TransactionRepository.posting_facts`): `statement_balance` = owed at the end of the latest statement day, `paid_since` = debits after it up to today, `spent_since` = credits after it, `bill` = statement less paid, never below zero, `owed_total` = owed today; entries dated ahead are not counted; nothing is stored. The card's page shows it under the balance (`partials/card_bill.html`, `.bill`: Due in N days in amber up to 5 days, Overdue in red, Paid, Nothing due; "Add the days" while unset) with **Pay bill** → `/transactions/new?pay=<account_id>&amount=<bill>&back=…` (the card is the To account, kind Transfer, the amount the total; **the account paid from is left empty**, nothing is filled in for the person). **The bell lists a bill due within `DUE_SOON_DAYS` (5) days or overdue (`TodoService`, `TodoRead.card_bills`, 2026-11-01).** Not built, and the owner does not want them now: recording the bank's statement and the difference, an assistant that matches a statement, autopay confirmation; a "set aside for card bills" line on Budgets is for later. Tests: `tests/test_card_bills.py`, `tests/test_migration_card_bill_days.py`, `tests/browser/test_card_bills.py`.

## Posting dates, rounded bills and the rows of the Add screen (2026-11-01, `feature/posting-date`)

**A line may carry a posting date** (`postings.posted_on`, migration `e1a3c5b7d9f2`; `PostingCreate.posted_on`, `PostingRead`, the backup (`BackupPosting.posted_on`, still version 5), the journal (`; posted: 2026-10-18` on a posting, `LedgerPosting.posted`), the assistants' `PostingIn.posted_on` and `_transaction` output, `label_payment`, `preview_update`): the day the account posts the line when that is not the entry's date (a card billing a purchase in a later statement: the owner's IGST lines). Never before the entry's date (`TransactionService._resolve(..., on=)`, and `_check_update` when only the date moves; the form puts a line back on the entry's day when the entry's date moves past it). **Only a card's bill reads it** (`AccountService.bill`: `posted_on or date`); balances, the budget, lists and reports keep using the entry's date. It is available on every account (the row's settings sheet shows the Date for any account, not for categories or in the recurring form), the account page shows "Posts 18 Oct" on a line that differs (`RegisterEntry.posted_on`) and the details sheet "posts 18 Oct" beside the account.

**A card's bill is rounded by the card's rule** (`accounts.bill_rounding` `none|nearest|up`, a CHECK; `BillRounding` in `schemas/account.py`; a select "Round the bill" on the account form; `update_account`/`create_account` take it; backup carry it). `cards.round_bill` rounds the statement balance to a whole unit (halves up for `nearest`); `CardBill` has `statement_balance` (exact), `statement_due` (rounded), `rounded_by` and `bill = max(0, statement_due - paid_since)`, so paying the rounded figure settles the bill and the paise left carry into the next statement. The card page shows a "Rounded" row when the rule changed the figure.

## Recurring transactions

A rule (`recurring_transactions`, `app/models/recurring.py`) is a transaction on a schedule, in one of two shapes: **simple** (from/to account and amount) or **split** (`recurring_postings` rows, like a transaction's postings ; the simple columns are then empty). The web form is the transaction form's rows (`partials/txn_rows.html`: total, From and To rows) and saves one account on each side as a simple rule, anything else as a split (`_parse` in `routers/web/recurring.py`); the API accepts either shape (`postings`, never mixed with from/to/amount). Both records go through `RecurringService._transaction`. Other fields: payee, note, `frequency` (daily/weekly/monthly/yearly) with `every` N, `start_date`, optional `end_date`, `next_date`, `last_date`, `active` and `last_error`. `services/schedule.py` is the pure date arithmetic (occurrence n = start plus n x every units, anchored to the start so a monthly rule on the 31st falls on the last day of short months). `RecurringService` (`services/recurring.py`) validates a rule with the same checks as recording it (`TransactionService.validate`), and `process_due()` records every occurrence on or before today through `TransactionService.create(..., recurring=(recurring_id, date))`, with `created_via="recurring"`. Each occurrence is recorded **once**: `transactions` has a unique index on `(recurring_id, recurring_date)`, so several workers or repeated visits are safe. Due occurrences are recorded in three places: when the user opens the dashboard, transactions or recurring pages (or lists/creates via the JSON API), and by `app/recurring_runner.py`, a background loop started in `main.lifespan` in every worker (`settings.recurring_interval_minutes`, default 15, 0 turns it off; the first run waits a minute). A rule that cannot be recorded (an account that can no longer be posted to) keeps its `last_error` and retries on the next run. **Skipping one occurrence** (`RecurringService.skip_next`, `POST /api/recurring/{recurring_id}/skip`, the "Skip <date>" button on the edit page, MCP `skip_recurring_occurrence`): nothing is recorded for the next occurrence and the rule moves on to the one after; the skipped day is kept in `skipped_date` (not `last_date`, which stays "last recorded"), so editing the rule continues after whichever of the two is later and never revives it; only a running rule can skip, it clears a stuck `last_error`, and skipping the last occurrence before `end_date` ends the rule. Pausing then resuming skips what fell due meanwhile; editing continues after the last recorded or skipped day; deleting keeps the recorded transactions (their link is cleared); an account used by a rule cannot be deleted. Web: `/recurring` (list, new, edit with pause/resume/delete); JSON API: `/api/recurring`; MCP: `list_recurring`, `create_recurring` (simple, or `postings` for a split), `stop_recurring`, `skip_recurring_occurrence`. "Today" follows `FINODE_TIMEZONE`.

**Related entries (2026-10-24).** `transactions.related_id` (migration `e7b9d1f3a5c8`, self-referencing, nullable, indexed) links an entry to an earlier one: a refund, reversal or cash-back, with no relation type yet (neutral wording: "Related to", "Related"). It is a link only: no amounts, balances, budget or reports read it. `TransactionCreate.related_id`, `TransactionUpdate.related_id` (null removes, leaving it out keeps) and `TransactionRead.related_id` carry it through the JSON API; `TransactionService._check_related` refuses an id that is not the person's own, an entry related to itself and a circle (`InvalidPostingError`, so a 400); `TransactionRepository.delete` clears what pointed at the deleted entry; `TransactionService.related(transaction_id)` lists the entries pointing at one. Deleting an entry unlinks what pointed at it. The backup carries `related_id` and a restore links last, mapped to the new ids. Web: the details sheet has **Related** (a text link beside Repeat; `/transactions/new?related=<transaction_id>`: a blank new entry that only carries the link, nothing copied from the original, 2026-10-24) and a "Related to" / "Related · N" list of rows that open the other entry's sheet in place (`related_info`); the form (`transaction_form.html`, `[data-related]`) has a hidden `related` field, a chip "Related to <payee · date · amount>" with ✕ and, with no link, a chip "Related" (link glyph, next to the date chip, 2026-10-26; renamed from "Transaction" 2026-11-04) that opens `<dialog id="related-picker">` (`GET /transactions/related-picker`, `partials/related_picker.html`, rows in `related_results.html`, the 30 newest entries up to today without Opening balance and Balance adjustment, `?q=` searches like the list's text search, `?results=1` answers only the rows, `?exclude=` leaves one out; `app.js` fills the chip on a tap, "related-picker-body" is one of `SHEET_BODIES`). The create and edit forms post `related` (empty clears it). **In the lists (2026-11-01) only the linked entry (the one that holds `related_id`) is marked, never the original:** after its payee a grey pill (`.tx-pill`, `partials/tx_title.html` macro `tx_title`) with the link glyph and the original's date and amount; the payee gives way first (ellipsis), the pill stays whole; tapping the pill opens the original's sheet (`hx-on:click` stops the row's own tap and its link), tapping the row opens its own. The date and amount come from `RelatedRef` (`schemas/transaction.py`), loaded in two queries by `TransactionRepository.related_refs` (none when nothing is related): `list_rows` in `routers/web/transactions.py` for Home, the Transactions list and "Show more", `RegisterEntry.related` for an account's and a category's page (also in the JSON registers). The mock-ups chose this over grouping under the original, which would have reordered days. The assistants set it with `related_to` on `record_transaction`, `record_split` and `update_transaction`, and `get_transaction` lists the entries pointing at one (2026-10-25).

**Tags are their own thing, apart from the note (2026-10-26).** Before this a tag was a `#word` in the note (`app/tags.py`, nothing stored). Now `tags` (`id`, `user_id`, `name`: lower case, 1 to 30 characters of letters, digits, `-`, `_`, at least one letter, unique per person) and `transaction_tags` (migration `a2c4e6f8b1d3`, which moved every existing tag out of the notes; its downgrade appends them back) hold them. **A tag exists only while an entry carries it** (`TransactionRepository.set_tags` drops unused ones). An entry has up to 10 tags (`MAX_TAGS_PER_ENTRY`), a person up to `FINODE_MAX_TAGS` (200, `TransactionService._check_tags`). `app/tags.py`: `clean_name`/`clean_names` make the one form (no `#`, lower case); `tags_in`, `without_tags`, `split_note` remain only for the migration and for backups before version 3.
